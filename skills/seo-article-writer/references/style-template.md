# House Style Template

Based on the author's sample article ("How to Make Money on eBay Without Selling Anything") and the final approved Appnable articles. Follow this unless the author says otherwise.

## Layout at a glance

```
Keyphrase: Make Money on eBay Without Selling Anything (used 7 times in the article)
Meta description: You might be familiar with selling things on eBay to make money. But do you
know how to make money on eBay without selling anything? Is it possible? Yes.
moneypantry.com

# How to Make Money on eBay Without Selling Anything        <- keyphrase built into the title

Intro paragraph 1: open with what the reader already knows, then a question that
leads to the keyphrase.
Intro paragraph 2: a statistic or two with the source named, then "To learn more,
read this article through to the end."

## About [Topic] / What Is [Keyphrase]?                       <- background
## Pros / Benefits of [Keyphrase]                              <- H3 sub-points
## Cons / Mistakes to Avoid                                    <- H3 sub-points or bold bullets
## How to [Keyphrase]                                          <- the main practical section
### 1. Method one
### 2. Method two
## Conclusion / Final Thoughts                                 <- keyphrase once + call to action
## FAQs                                                        <- 3-5 questions as H3s
## References                                                  <- numbered, hyperlinked
```

Note: in the sample, FAQs come after the conclusion. In the approved Appnable articles, FAQs come before "Final Thoughts". Either is acceptable; follow whichever the author prefers, defaulting to FAQs before Final Thoughts so the article ends on the call to action and references.

## Voice

- **Plain and friendly.** Write the way a helpful colleague explains something. Short sentences, everyday words, no jargon without a quick explanation.
- **Talk to the reader.** Use "you" and "your team". Ask the occasional question ("Is it possible, you may ask? Yes it is!").
- **Keep them reading.** Short bridge lines between sections are part of the house style: "Keep reading to see how…", "Before we get to…, let's look at…". Use 2–3 per article, not after every section.
- **Transitions.** Also, Furthermore, Additionally, However, In fact. Vary them; don't start consecutive paragraphs with the same one.
- **Short paragraphs.** 2–4 sentences. One idea per paragraph.
- **Lists with substance.** Bullets and numbered steps carry a bold lead-in followed by a full sentence of explanation.
- **Facts with sources.** Name the source in the sentence ("According to Thomson Reuters…") and back it with a "Read also" link and a reference entry.
- **Close with action.** The conclusion recaps the main point in 3–4 sentences, uses the keyphrase once, then a 1–2 sentence call to action for the company.

## Quality fixes over the sample

The sample has some habits not to copy:

- Conflicting or outdated statistics (e.g., two different user counts). Use one current, sourced figure.
- Raw domain names as references ("zeen.com"). Always give publisher, page title and the full hyperlinked URL.
- Long detours unrelated to the keyphrase (e.g., a third-party integration plug). Keep every section serving the reader's question.
- Year stamps in headings ("|2022"). Only add a year if the author asks; it dates the article.

## "Read also" line format

```
**Also read:** [Title of the Linked Page](https://full-url)
**Read also:** [Title of the Linked Page](https://full-url)
```

One per line, directly under the paragraph it supports. Four per article, alternating the two lead-ins.

## Reference list format

```
1. Publisher, "Page title." https://full-url
2. Publisher, "Page title" (report/press release, Month Year). https://full-url
```
