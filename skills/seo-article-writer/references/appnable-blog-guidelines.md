# Appnable Blog Writing Guidelines (from Marketing)

Source: `Appnable_Blog_Writing_Guidelines.pdf` in this folder. Every Appnable blog must follow these rules, whoever writes it. If a draft fails one checklist item, it is not ready to publish. If this summary and the PDF ever disagree, the PDF wins.

## Pre-publish checklist

Copy this list into each draft (on its own page at the end of the Word document).

- ☐ 1,500 words or fewer (body + FAQs)
- ☐ High-ranking keywords (long-tail or short-tail) researched with the Claude keyword research skill, relevant to the blog
- ☐ One primary keyword chosen, and included in the title, URL and first 100 words
- ☐ Remaining researched keywords used naturally in the body
- ☐ No keyword stuffing; a final read confirms all chosen keywords are included naturally and none were missed
- ☐ Title under 60 characters; meta description 140–160 characters
- ☐ UK English throughout, with Grammarly or other writing tools set to UK English
- ☐ Spelling, punctuation and terminology consistent throughout
- ☐ Reflects Appnable's brand voice and expertise; the message is accurate and not misrepresented
- ☐ Every statistic, quote and data point hyperlinked to its original source where it appears in the text (no reference list at the bottom)
- ☐ One internal link to an Appnable Solution page
- ☐ Two FAQs, each answer ending with a link to the website's FAQ page
- ☐ Two or three "Read also" links to related Appnable blogs, once suitable articles are available (can be left out of the first one or two blogs)
- ☐ One clear call to action (book a demo, download a lead magnet, or contact us)
- ☐ Every image compressed and not high definition
- ☐ Technical claims checked by a subject-matter expert
- ☐ Proofread, and read aloud once for flow

## 1. Length and structure

1,000–1,500 words target; 1,500 maximum (body + FAQs). Order:

1. Title (H1): one per blog, contains the primary keyword
2. Introduction: 80–120 words; the problem and what the reader will learn; primary keyword in first 100 words
3. Body: 3–5 H2 sections; H3s for sub-points; never skip a level
4. Conclusion: 60–100 words, ending with the call to action
5. Read also: 2–3 related blogs
6. FAQs: at least two
7. Sources: no reference list; every source hyperlinked where used

Paragraphs of 3–4 sentences; sentences under 25 words where possible; bullets or a short table for steps, options, comparisons.

## 2. Keywords

One primary keyword and 2–4 supporting keywords per blog, all tied to a service Appnable sells. Use the Claude keyword research skill to find high-ranking keywords and confirm search volume.

| Service area | Primary keyword | Supporting keywords |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft 365 / Copilot | Microsoft Copilot consultant UK | Copilot readiness assessment, Copilot data security, Microsoft 365 consultancy |
| Automation | business process automation UK | workflow automation for regulated industries, Power Automate consultant, AI automation platform |
| Custom software | custom software development UK | bespoke business software, software development for financial services, legal tech development |
| ERP | ERP implementation UK | ERP consultancy, Dynamics 365 implementation, replacing spreadsheets with ERP |
| Compliance-led technology | technology consultancy for regulated industries | GDPR compliant software, data governance consultancy, digital transformation for law firms |

Primary keyword must appear in: the title (ideally near the start), the URL (e.g. `/blog/copilot-readiness-assessment`), and the first 100 words.

Rules: write for the reader first; if a keyword sounds forced, rephrase or drop it. Don't repeat the exact keyword more than 4–5 times in a 1,500-word post; use natural variations. Never target the same primary keyword in two blogs; log each blog's primary keyword in the content tracker.

## 3. Sources

No source, no claim. Hyperlink each statistic, quote and data point to its original source where used ("Over half of UK firms now use some form of AI, according to ONS data."); links open in a new tab. No References section. Confirm each source supports the claim. Credible sources only: ONS, ICO, FCA, gov.uk, Microsoft Learn, peer-reviewed research, Gartner, McKinsey, Forrester, reputable press. Data no older than three years unless historical; say the year. Link the original report, not a report quoting it. Quote sparingly, in quotation marks, credited by name and role. Avoid competitor blogs, Wikipedia, AI-generated summaries and unsourced statistics.

## 4. "Read also" links

2–3 related Appnable blogs, chosen by relevance (same service area, logical next step, or deeper dive). Two placements: one inline mid-blog where it fits, plus a block of 2–3 after the conclusion. When a new blog publishes, add a Read also link to it from one or two older related blogs. Can be left out of the first one or two blogs. Check quarterly that links work and are still the best match.

## 5. FAQs

At least two. Real questions (sales calls, comments, Google "People also ask" for the primary keyword), written as a reader would type them. Answer in 40–60 words, directly in the first sentence, ending with "See more on our [FAQ page](FAQ page URL)." The question should also exist on the FAQ page; if not, send it to the web admin. Use a supporting keyword in at least one question. (Google mostly shows FAQ rich results for government and health sites; FAQs still help readers, search, AI answer engines and FAQ page traffic.)

## 6. Additional standards

- **Title:** under 60 characters, primary keyword near the start, a clear benefit ("Copilot Readiness: 5 Checks Before You Switch It On").
- **URL slug:** short, lowercase, hyphens, keyword only; no dates or stop words.
- **Internal links:** at least one to a relevant Solution page, descriptive anchor text.
- **Images:** one featured image plus helpful visuals; descriptive alt text; under 200 KB each.
- **Call to action:** one per blog, matched to the topic.
- **Technical accuracy:** engineer/SME reviews every technical claim, step and screenshot; name versions and dates ("as of October 2026"); explain each acronym on first use.
- **Style:** for a busy decision-maker; lead with the answer; plain UK English; active voice; no clichés, filler or AI-sounding copy; examples from legal, financial services, pharmaceuticals and government; never name a client or share client data without written approval.
- **Review:** two reviews before publishing: technical, and marketing against this checklist.

## Author additions (these apply on top of Marketing's guidelines)

- **UK English** throughout, because Appnable is a UK company.
- **Transitions:** natural transitions where they genuinely link ideas (However, Still, That said, In practice, For example); never forced, never the same twice in a row; no filler (Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally). See `humanizing-rules.md`.
- **Humanizing:** every blog goes through the two-pass humanizing edit in `humanizing-rules.md`. No generic copy.
- **Mid-blog selling section:** every blog has one, tied to a relevant Appnable service, product or case study (see `appnable-company.md`).
- **Statistics phrasing:** where a statistic is needed, write "According to stats by [Source], ..." with the hyperlink on the words "stats by [Source]". Vary the wording if a blog has several statistics ("[Source]'s 2025 figures show ...", still linking the source name), so the phrase doesn't become a pattern.
- **Images:** no AI-generated images; only the sources in `image-sources.md`; record the cover image source in every blog.
- **Products:** StGatherly and KoKoodi are Appnable's own products; Pendroply is a client-based app.

## Open questions for Marketing

- **ERP keywords:** the keyword table lists "Dynamics 365 implementation", but Appnable implements **Odoo** (appnable.com/solutions). Until Marketing confirms, don't target Dynamics 365 or claim Appnable implements it; use Odoo-based keywords (e.g. "Odoo implementation UK") instead.
- **FAQ page:** none found on appnable.com yet. Ask the author for the URL.
- **Solution pages:** all five services are on one page (/solutions/) for now.
- **Client names:** the website names some clients. Only reuse case studies exactly as published on the website; never add names or figures beyond it without written approval.
